# Editorial policy

## Mission

Turn relevant technology events into practical guidance connected to d.d.c. services. The audience is primarily Russian-speaking businesses, product owners, operators, and teams in Russia, without excluding useful international developments.

## Topic mix

- 35% AI and AI-enabled automation.
- 25% web and online-platform development.
- 20% design, UX, and awards.
- 10% product and MVP.
- 10% security, support, SEO, analytics, and audit.

Treat percentages as editorial guidance, not quotas.

## Sports and platforms priority

Actively seek concrete AI and development applications for:

- sports venues, arenas, clubs, schools, and federations;
- bookings, schedules, memberships, ticketing, and online payments;
- occupancy and demand forecasting;
- support bots and knowledge assistants;
- CRM, access control, notifications, and staff workflows;
- video analysis, coaching, safety, maintenance, and fan engagement;
- multi-role online platforms and marketplaces.

Do not claim an application works merely because a vendor announces it. Look for implementation details, limitations, or measured outcomes.

## Candidate score

Score each item before drafting:

| Criterion | Points |
| --- | ---: |
| Connection to a d.d.c. service | 0–3 |
| Practical value | 0–3 |
| Source reliability | 0–3 |
| Freshness | 0–2 |
| Relevance to Russian-speaking audience | 0–2 |
| Opportunity for original analysis | 0–2 |
| Promotional bias | 0 to −2 |
| Duplicate of a recent post | 0 to −3 |

Normally shortlist candidates scoring 10 or more. A lower score may pass only with a stated editorial reason.

## Editorial formats

- **Brief analysis:** 300–600 words.
- **Practical explainer:** 800–1500 words.
- **Award/project review:** problem, solution, transferable lesson, and limits.
- **Digest:** 3–5 developments, each with one d.d.c. conclusion.

Default cadence: one main article and one brief or digest per week. Quality outranks frequency.

## Hard gates

Reject or pause a post when:

- no primary source can be found;
- the event date is unclear;
- the topic cannot be connected to a real audience decision;
- the central claim is a rumor or vendor claim presented as independent fact;
- metrics or testimonials cannot be verified;
- the content substantially duplicates an existing post;
- publication would require copying protected text or imagery.
